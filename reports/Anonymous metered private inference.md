# Anonymous metered inference needs a narrower claim

> Review added 6 November 2026: see the [independent feedback](Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference%20-%20review.md) for corrections and qualifications, and the [proposed architecture](../ARCHITECTURE.md) for the recommended next steps. The original report below is retained for comparison.

> Update added 6 November 2026: act-rs was built and tested after the user approved it, so the statements below that it was never built or run are out of date, and validation row 5 is partly done. It passed its 32 upstream tests and the credit scenarios on the same M5 Max laptop, with a 32-bit spend measured at 4,531 bytes and about 9 ms to verify, roughly six times the older crate's CPU cost. x86 timings and the draft's unfinished security arguments remain open. See the [act-rs test](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/spike_act_rs.md). The text below is otherwise unchanged.

The underlying concepts are sound enough for a private proof of concept, but the claim that only the purchase is non-private is false as written and should not be made. Top-ups, refunds, disputes and tax records identify the buyer again, and no design helps against someone with physical custody of the server or someone watching both ends of the Tor path. Two concepts stand on solid footing: Tor for hiding the client's IP address, and attested inference on Tinfoil's own fleet, though the second depends on an AMD patch check that has no published values today. Four are conditional: hidden-balance credits with returned change, a pre-debit token hold, verifiable self-hosting on Verda, and separating a card purchase from the credit it funds. The rest is weak or missing: Tor as traffic-shape hiding, exact per-token amounts alongside privacy, the AMD patch check, a safe home for the credit issuer's secret key, anonymity at small user counts, and the composition itself, since no test joined any two layers. Every code test ran on an M5 Max laptop, nothing ran on Verda hardware or a GPU, and the recommended credential library, act-rs, was never built because a permission check refused it. The step that most reduces uncertainty is about 1.5 hours on a Verda spot instance for about $5, followed by a 1-2 day custom-image test that decides whether Verda can be a verifiable backend at all.

## Tor and attestation hold, but the purchase-only claim fails twice

Three labels mark evidence throughout. **Tested** means code ran in this research, always on a laptop, so results may not transfer to an x86 confidential VM. **Read** means taken from a primary source without running it. **Inference** means reasoning, arithmetic or simulation over an invented population. An *attestation* is a hardware-signed statement of what booted, and a *launch measurement* is the SHA-384 digest of firmware, kernel, initramfs and command line that an AMD SEV-SNP chip signs into that statement. A *confidential VM* (CVM) runs with memory encrypted against the host, "CC" in Verda and NVIDIA product names means confidential computing, and GPU-CC is NVIDIA's mode for it. An *onion service* is a server reachable only through Tor, with no public address exposed to clients. *EC-ACT* (elliptic-curve Anonymous Credit Tokens) is the draft credit protocol explained in the next section.

**The claim fails in two separate ways.** Purchase is not the only identified moment, because refunds, disputes, EU withdrawal rights and VAT records touch identity again. Scale also cannot help against a both-ends observer, against which Tor's own design paper says it "does not protect" ([Source](https://svn-archive.torproject.org/svn/projects/design-paper/tor-design.html)), or against physical custody. TEE.fail used an interposer under $1000 plus root to forge a quote on Intel TDX and recover an OpenSSL key on SNP ([Source](https://tee.fail/)), and AMD treats comparable attacks as outside the SNP threat model ([AMD-SB-3048](https://www.amd.com/en/resources/product-security/bulletin/amd-sb-3048.html)). A server break does not unblind past spends or reveal IPs, though it does expose future activity (inference, [red team](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/red_team.md)).

The strongest wording this research supports is ([red team](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/red_team.md)):

> When you buy credits, Stripe, your card issuer and the operator know who you are, when you bought and how much, and they keep those records. Refunds and disputes identify you again.
>
> After purchase, the credit protocol is designed so that a spend has no cryptographic link to your purchase or your other spends. That design has no published proof or audit yet. Tor hides your IP address from the operator, the datacentre and the model provider. Your prompts are encrypted to published, attested code, and you can check what code ran. Nobody can read them remotely or through software unless they exploit a flaw in that code or in the processor's firmware, and today that check cannot confirm every known firmware flaw is patched.
>
> This does not protect you from anyone watching both your connection and the server, or from anyone with physical access to the server. It does not hide your usage pattern from the datacentre. It does not protect you from the operator releasing a bad version before someone notices. Until many people use the service at the same time, the operator may be able to guess which customer you are from timing and amounts. You pay in rounded amounts; your device shows the exact tokens.

Below a few hundred active users, add: "Today, treat this as content privacy and IP hiding. It is not anonymity." **Small scale is a statistical failure, not a cryptographic one.** If an operator can recognise a first spend, its best guess at the buyer is right with probability 1.00 at 1 buyer, 0.23 at 100 and under 0.01 at 10,000, assuming a 24-hour mean delay. Buying and using within an hour gives 0.88 at 100 buyers, while holding a week's reserve gives 0.04, so buying ahead is the cheapest strong lever ([purchase notes](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/purchase_wallet_abuse.md)). These are simulation results over invented populations. Operator-published user counts may lengthen a delay but should never remove one, because the operator can inflate any count.

## Most pieces exist, but the hold, the card join and the Verda image are new

**No single system found has all of it, so the novelty is the composition** ([existing systems](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/existing_systems.md)). Apple and Meta use anonymous credentials for authorisation and rate limits, not token billing ([Apple](https://security.apple.com/blog/private-cloud-compute/), [Meta](https://ai.meta.com/static-resource/private-processing-technical-whitepaper)). OpenPCC is the most complete public specification of credits with spend and refund on attested inference ([Source](https://github.com/openpcc/openpcc)), but its banks are in-memory only, so durable double-spend state must be built ([Source](https://github.com/openpcc/openpcc/tree/main/anonpay/banking)). Routstr is the closest live system, and it takes Bitcoin only ([Source](https://github.com/Routstr/routstr-core)). Two systems can spend per session rather than per request: zkAPI's October 2026 build, which Open Anonymity led with the Ethereum Foundation, and Open Anonymity's fixed blind-RSA tickets, issued per prompt or per session ([zkAPI](https://blog.ethereum.org/2026/10/01/introducing-zkapi), [Open Anonymity](https://openanonymity.ai/blog/posts/unlinkable-inference.md)). In zkAPI the model provider still sees prompts, and the zkAPI server learns dollars per session. Audits of the reference systems found the seams this design inherits: Trail of Bits flagged Meta's multi-use credentials and issuance-to-use timing ([Source](https://github.com/trailofbits/publications/raw/master/reviews/2025-08-meta-whatsapp-privateprocessing-securityreview.pdf)), and researchers reused an Apple one-time token "between 2 and 5 times" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.24239)).

**"Zero-knowledge proofs" here are lighter than the phrase suggests.** EC-ACT (elliptic-curve Anonymous Credit Tokens) gives the client a signed hidden balance. At each spend the client proves it can afford the amount and reveals a *nullifier*, a one-time public serial that lets the server refuse a double spend without learning whose credit it was, then receives its change as a fresh credit. The proofs are sigma protocols, so no SNARK, circuit or trusted setup enters the stack ([Source](https://moderation-of-unlinkable-endorsements.github.io/internet-drafts/draft-schlesinger-mole-ec-act.html)). They prove balance arithmetic only. Nothing in the stack proves the enclave counted tokens correctly ([credential notes](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/credentials_crypto.md)).

**"HTTP/DNS" credential storage is undefined in this research.** No note defines the phrase, and no note treats DNS as a credential transport. This report reads it as a clearnet path and treats it as exposed, so any DNS-based mechanism remains unresearched. The separation between that path and the Tor spend path is testable by packet capture, and it must cover cookies, TLS tickets, resolver queries and telemetry ([purchase notes](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/purchase_wallet_abuse.md)).

**Start on RTX PRO 6000, because B200 CC rents only as an 8-GPU AMD machine at $74.57 per hour.** The logged-in console shows `1RTXPRO6000.30V.CC` at $2.78 per hour, or $1.39 listed spot, on SEV-SNP in FIN-03. No 1x B200 exists, the $9.32 figure is a per-GPU price, 2x and 4x showed no available row, and the Intel B200 has no location ([console notes](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/verda_browser_notes.md)). RTX PRO runs single-GPU only, and NVIDIA ships no cryptographic binding between GPU and CVM on either SKU, because Blackwell GPU-CC relies on encrypted bounce buffers ([attestation notes](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/tee_gpu_attestation.md)). The monthly floor is about $2,085 before licence, counsel, fees and maintainer time ([red team](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/red_team.md)). **No measured performance figure covers RTX PRO on SNP.** The nearest figures are about 10% on RTX PRO under TDX and 34-39% on one B200 under TDX with SGLang ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23969), [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26575)).

**Tinfoil is two different integrations, and neither carries over to Verda.** The default verifier rejects machines outside Tinfoil's inventory, and `cvmimage` "needs Tinfoil host access" ([Source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tinfoilsh/cvmimage/main/AGENTS.md)). Tinfoil Containers run a customer's image inside Tinfoil-operated enclaves, GPU access requires contacting Tinfoil, the config must live in a public repository, and self-hosting is not mentioned ([Source](https://docs.tinfoil.sh/containers/overview.md)). That route keeps the metering gate in attested code. Pooled-key hosted inference is documented ([Source](https://docs.tinfoil.sh/guides/proxy-server.md)) but weaker. Tinfoil then sees per-request timing, model, token counts and gateway IP, and the usage figure arrives as an unsigned header ([Source](https://docs.tinfoil.sh/admin/admin-api.md)). Its limits are soft, so the proxy must enforce the cap itself, and a pooled key shares one prompt cache unless each request uses a fresh secret ([Source](https://docs.tinfoil.sh/sdk/prompt-caching.md)). Each backend should carry its own label.

**Maintainability conflicts with the component count.** The minimum list spans a Stripe path, issuer, unaudited credential code, atomic store, gate, engine, Tor service, CPU and GPU verifiers, reproducible image, wallet with journal and backend adapters. The journal, single configuration and verifier cannot be cut, while log independence, multiple models and multiple backends can wait if the claims shrink to match ([red team](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/red_team.md)).

### Research conduct note

Four things went outside plan. **Tor port collision:** the Tor harness hard-coded a port already held by the attestation test's local server, so for at most 53 seconds onion port 80 forwarded to that server. Logs show 4 stray requests from the spike's own client, and the address was known only locally, which logs cannot prove. The identity was deleted, the harness was fixed, no measurements come from that window, and attestation results may include those requests ([Tor test](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/spike_tor.md)). **GitHub login:** early read-only `gh api` metadata calls used the user's existing login before the test switched to unauthenticated requests, and nothing was written to GitHub ([attestation test](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/spike_attestation.md)). `gh auth status` also ran once ([metering test](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/spike_metering.md)).

**Files outside the sandbox:** `gh attestation verify` wrote a Sigstore cache to `~/.cache/gh/.sigstore`. After checking every entry came from that run, the test removed only that subtree, though the parent directory's modification time changed. A plain `go version` may have touched Go's telemetry counters in `~/Library/Application Support/go/telemetry`. Attribution is uncertain, and those files were left in place rather than removed. Two empty files from shell typos in `/tmp` are gone. **Unverified signatures:** llama.cpp's Sigstore signature could not be checked (`unsupported tlog public key type: PKIX_ED25519`, gh 2.49.1), though its SHA-256 matched GitHub's digest and the SLSA subject. PyPI attestations were read but not verified, the model file was not reproduced from upstream weights, and Arti's tag signature was good from a key outside the web of trust. Raw spike evidence sits in session scratch space and may not persist. The [fact-check](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/fact_check.md) covered 20 claims selected by impact, read two notes only in part, and did not review the red team note. The tests ran concurrently on one machine, which may have affected timings and stalls. The user's own AI-produced investigation of 25 October 2026 arrived after the research tracks and tests had finished, so none of them used it. It was treated as a source of ideas, not evidence, and checked separately: of 24 claim rows, 11 were confirmed, 11 partly right, 1 wrong and 1 could not be verified ([copy](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/prior_investigation_2026_10_25.md), [review](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/prior_investigation_review.md)).

## The joins between layers carry the largest unmeasured risks

**One person holds every root.** The sole operator controls the buyer list, Stripe, Verda, image releases, client releases, configuration and price table. A malicious release defeats confidentiality and metering together with no hardware attack, and transparency logs give evidence only afterwards and only if someone independent watches ([red team](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/red_team.md)). Cure53 rated a frontend that never compared the measurement as High (CVSS 8.7), which shows how easily this control goes missing ([Source](https://cure53.de/pentest-report_expressvpn-ai_2026.pdf)).

**The issuer secret key has no good home.** In EC-ACT the same key verifies spends and mints value. Verda's encrypted-disk flow has the operator unlock after each restart, while RAM-only keys void balances at reboot, and the editor's copy of the protocol draft says balances "do not carry over to a new context" ([Source](https://moderation-of-unlinkable-endorsements.github.io/internet-drafts/draft-jms-mole-protocols.html)). Updates add pressure: spot instances "may be evicted without warning", and the service must change image whenever Tor ships a security release. Rollback is a second hole, since the spend store lives outside the enclave, no memory freshness or monotonic counter is documented, and restoring an old copy revives spent credits. Minting damage is bounded by $2.78 per hour of displaced capacity on a self-hosted GPU, but on Tinfoil it costs real upstream cash ([red team](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/red_team.md)).

**"Per token" and "private" cannot both be exact.** The spend amount encodes prompt size, and the returned change encodes output length. Rounding to multiples of 256 raises the median number of users sharing an amount from 1 to 28 at 100 active users, at a 7.6% mean overcharge ([credential notes](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/credentials_crypto.md)). OpenPCC reaches a similar place with 512 values at most 6.25% apart ([Source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openpcc/openpcc/main/anonpay/currency/value.go)). Settlement therefore has to be coarse, with only the local ledger showing exact tokens. Prefix caching links requests and must be off ([Source](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/gu25b.html)), so a stateless 20-turn conversation at 200 tokens per turn bills 42,000 input tokens against 4,000 with reuse.

**Spending once per session is the main alternative to a per-request hold, and it is untested.** The user's 25 October investigation proposed one spend to open a session, a balance held in enclave memory, and change returned at close, with uniform credit chains and only two spend amounts. This removes per-request amounts from the credit protocol and needs no settlement trip per turn. However, the change at close still shows the session's cost to whoever holds the issuer key, single-turn sessions keep a rounded per-request leak, turns within a session are linked by design, and hiding the amount from the operator rests on the enclave. The client's check also weakens: its per-token display becomes the enclave's report, compared against one rounded figure, and a lost refund can strand the rest of a chain. Both options need a custom settlement flow, so the protocol draft does not separate them. This is the leading candidate to test, not a settled answer (inference, [review](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/prior_investigation_review.md)).

Other ideas from that investigation divide cleanly. Two are cheap and worth keeping: binding the onion and inner encryption keys into attestation report data, and logging issuer keys and contexts so a per-user key is visible, which matters because the credit test's client accepted a per-user context. Weekly purchase cohorts, paid cover sessions, three-provider custody and witness quorums should stay out of any PoC claim. The check found cohorts unjustified at this scale, and the investigation's look-alike table, 20-35% engine cost and per-token cost are model output or unsourced, so they are not repeated here ([review](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/prior_investigation_review.md)).

**Metering integrity is more exposed than confidentiality, though confidentiality has the higher stakes.** A hash chain proves consistency and not honesty, as the 3.27x test showed. A server that records the protocol's `NoUpdate` outcome ends the credit chain and takes the remaining balance "with no cryptographic recourse" ([credential notes](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/credentials_crypto.md)). Anyone able to kill a stream can force a retry that re-presents the same nullifier on a fresh circuit, which turns recovery into a probe of about 5 KB ([red team](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/red_team.md)). The hold also deviates from the current protocol draft, whose server "MUST NOT attempt the protected operation unless this request's result was successfully recorded", so settling after inference needs a custom two-phase flow ([Source](https://moderation-of-unlinkable-endorsements.github.io/internet-drafts/draft-jms-mole-protocols.html)). A usable check exists: the client can reproduce prompt counts exactly and completion counts in aggregate (16,596 against 16,347, a 1.5% gap), enough to catch sustained skew above about 5%, on one small model on llama.cpp only.

**The host sits underneath every privacy layer.** Verda sees all CVM packets, bounce-buffer activity, block I/O and the serial console, and it can delay packets after any shaper runs ([red team](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/red_team.md)). It keeps infrastructure logs and job activity "Typically up to 12 months" ([Source](https://verda.com/privacy-policy)). A study on H100 measured size-dependent GPU transfer timing ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02770)), but no paper shows token or length recovery from the host on Blackwell, so this remains plausible and undemonstrated. A Verda tenant cannot capture from the host, so testing it needs a host you control, and a fixed-shape model engine to close the channel would be new engineering with no measured cost ([review](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/prior_investigation_review.md)). Whisper Leak classified prompt topics from encrypted stream metadata at above 98% AUPRC for 17 of 28 models, and at about 46-85% from timing alone, with no Tor test ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03675)). Carlini and Nasr report that a constant output rate "will thwart our attacks completely" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.17175v1)), but output token totals alone still leak ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15431)), and start and stop times remain visible. Constant-rate sending was not tested here. At one Tor cell per frame, 100 ms frames cost about 41 kbit/s and 250 ms frames about 16 kbit/s, nothing yet favours either, and network stalls would still reach the client as bursts (inference, [review](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/prior_investigation_review.md)).

**Denominations deserve a real comparison before EC-ACT is assumed.** Rejecting an invalid spend proof cost 1.5 ms on the older crate, against 0.38 microseconds to generate one ([credit test](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/spike_act.md)). At the interpolated EC-ACT cost, about 116 invalid spends per second saturate a core, or roughly one onion circuit's throughput, so 12-42 circuits could saturate all 30 vCPUs (inference, [red team](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/red_team.md)). Blind RSA rejects invalid tokens about 400 times more cheaply than interpolated EC-ACT, separates verifying from signing, and risks only the tokens being spent rather than a whole balance. Its costs are bandwidth and change handling: RSA-2048 needs 2,816 bytes each way and about 30 ms of signing for 11 denominations ([credential notes](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/credentials_crypto.md)).

## Conclusion

Viability and anonymity do not arrive together. At an illustrative $10 per month, about 209 buyers cover the $2,085 floor, yet the first-spend best guess at that size is still 0.78 with an hour's delay and 0.13 with a day's. A service can pay its bills while its anonymity claim remains weak, so "confidential; weakly anonymous beta" is the accurate label well past launch. The honest boundary for this proof of concept is a Stripe sandbox plus private use, which avoids nearly all the legal exposure and still proves the mechanics ([red team](../research_notes/Anonymous%20metered%20private%20inference/red_team.md)).

Two technology bets look different after this research than they do on paper. EC-ACT's distinguishing feature is exact private change, but privacy forces rounding anyway, so denominations may give up less than expected while removing the issuer key's minting exposure from the verifier, the junk-spend cost and the whole-balance risk. And the Verda test is a backend decision as much as a security one: if the custom image digest does not match, Tinfoil Containers become the near-term route to attested metering, at the price of a second trust domain and a dependency on a single vendor's fleet.
