running

xtask generates the operator's keys, signs a release, starts every service, attests and unlocks the gate, and signs its fresh issuer key into the manifest. C tor publishes the gate as an onion service, and walletd bootstraps its own Tor client (Arti).

    1 Approve the manifest

    waiting

    The wallet fetches the operator-signed manifest from the storefront and checks it against the operator key pinned in wallet.toml. A new manifest is only trusted once you approve it.

    
    
    

    
    
      
    
        
    2 Buy credit
    
        waiting
      
    
      
    The wallet blinds a fresh credential request, journals it, and opens an order. You pay on the
        checkout page; the storefront then has the gate sign the blinded request, and the wallet unblinds it. The
        storefront and the gate saw the payment, never the credential the wallet ends up holding.
    
      
    
      
    
      
      
    
      

    
      
      
      
        
      3 Chat over Tor
      
        waiting
      
      
      
      Each conversation opens a session: walletd spends part of a credential to the gate, unlinkably,
        and the gate meters every token against it. Closing the session refunds the rest as a fresh credential.
        Each session gets its own Tor circuits, so its first message waits for a fresh path to
        the onion service: usually 5 to 30 seconds.
      
      
      
      
      
        
        
      
      
      
      
        
      The fake engine answers with NATO-alphabet words, a deterministic
          stand-in for a real model, streamed token by token.
